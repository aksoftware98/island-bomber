    How bombs work

    When you let go of a bomb it keeps your jet's speed and direction. After that only gravity acts on it (g = 9.81 m/s²), pulling it down faster and faster. The path is a curve called a parabola. So you have to let go before you are over the target.

    Fall time t = √(2h / g)
    Distance it travels forward d = v · t

    Example: flying level at 40 m/s, 45 m above the truck. t = √(90 / 9.81) ≈ 3.0 s, so the bomb lands about 40 × 3.0 ≈ 121 m ahead of where you let go.

    Climbing throws the bomb upward first, so it falls for longer and flies further. Diving does the opposite. Faster or higher means you must let go earlier. The Flight physics panel shows all of this live, including how far you are from the nearest truck.

    Trucks drive around, so aim where the truck will be when the bomb lands. A bomb within 14 m destroys a truck. A near miss within 22 m damages it, and a second near miss finishes it. Trucks stop to shoot back when you are close. Out of bombs? Fly low through the green ring on the runway to reload and patch up.
